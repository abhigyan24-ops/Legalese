# Rights Quest — Research Citations & Data Sources (`SOURCES.md`)

All statistical facts and "Did You Know?" callouts in Rights Quest are sourced from official statutory reports, government censuses, and verified international development publications. No statistics are invented.

## 1. Right to Education (Article 21-A & RTE Act 2009)

* **Stat 1**: *"Under Section 12(1)(c) of the RTE Act 2009, all private unaided schools must reserve at least 25% of entry-level seats for children from Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) and disadvantaged groups for free education."*
  * **Source**: *Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 (Gazette of India, Act No. 35 of 2009), Section 12(1)(c).*
* **Stat 2**: *"Over 14.89 lakh schools and 26.5 crore students are covered under India's unified educational monitoring framework, with elementary transition rates exceeding 93%."*
  * **Source**: *Unified District Information System for Education Plus (UDISE+) 2021–22 Report, Department of School Education and Literacy, Ministry of Education, Government of India.*
* **Stat 3**: *"Section 14 of the RTE Act explicitly mandates that no child shall be denied school admission for lack of age proof or birth certificate."*
  * **Source**: *Ministry of Education Guidelines on RTE Act Section 14 & Section 16 (Prohibition of Detention).*

## 2. Right to Healthcare (Article 21 & National Health Mission)

* **Stat 1**: *"Under the Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK), over 25 crore children aged 0–18 are screened across government schools and Anganwadi centres for 4Ds: Defects at birth, Deficiencies, Diseases, and Developmental delays."*
  * **Source**: *Operational Guidelines on Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK), Child Health Division, Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (MoHFW), Government of India.*
* **Stat 2**: *"Full immunization coverage among Indian children aged 12–23 months increased to 76.4% nationally under Mission Indradhanush."*
  * **Source**: *National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5) 2019–21, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of India.*
* **Stat 3**: *"Article 21 of the Constitution has been interpreted by the Supreme Court of India (Paschim Banga Khet Mazdoor Samity v. State of West Bengal, 1996) to hold that the right to emergency medical treatment is an inviolable fundamental right."*
  * **Source**: *Supreme Court of India Case Law: 1996 SCC (4) 37.*

## 3. Protection from Child Labour (CLPRA 1986 / Amendment 2016)

* **Stat 1**: *"The Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act 2016 completely prohibits the employment of children below 14 years in all commercial occupations, and prohibits adolescents (14–18) from hazardous occupations."*
  * **Source**: *The Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2016 (Act No. 35 of 2016), Ministry of Labour and Employment, Government of India.*
* **Stat 2**: *"According to Census of India, the total number of working children aged 5–14 was estimated at 4.35 million, representing a substantial decline from 12.6 million in 2001."*
  * **Source**: *Census of India (Office of the Registrar General & Census Commissioner, India) & Ministry of Labour and Employment Annual Report.*
* **Stat 3**: *"Penalties for employing a child under 14 were raised in 2016 to include mandatory imprisonment of 6 months up to 2 years and fines from ₹20,000 to ₹50,000."*
  * **Source**: *Section 14, Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act 1986 (as amended 2016).*

## 4. Protection from Abuse (POCSO Act 2012)

* **Stat 1**: *"The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act 2012 defines any person below 18 years as a child, mandates gender-neutral protection, and establishes child-friendly Special Courts."*
  * **Source**: *The POCSO Act, 2012 (Act No. 32 of 2012), Ministry of Women and Child Development, Government of India.*
* **Stat 2**: *"Section 23 of the POCSO Act strictly criminalizes the disclosure of the victim's identity or photograph in any media, punishable with up to one year imprisonment."*
  * **Source**: *POCSO Act 2012, Section 23 & 2019 Amendment Rules, Ministry of Women and Child Development.*
* **Stat 3**: *"Childline 1098 operates 24/7 across 600+ districts in India, answering over 5 million distress calls from and concerning children each year."*
  * **Source**: *Childline India Foundation (CIF) & National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR) Annual Report 2022–23.*

## 5. Protection from Child Marriage (PCMA 2006)

* **Stat 1**: *"Under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006 (PCMA), the legal minimum age of marriage is strictly 18 years for females and 21 years for males."*
  * **Source**: *The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 (Act No. 6 of 2007), Section 2(a).*
* **Stat 2**: *"According to NFHS-5 (2019–21), the percentage of women aged 20–24 married before age 18 dropped to 23.3%, down from 26.8% in NFHS-4 and 47.4% in NFHS-3."*
  * **Source**: *National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5) 2019–21, International Institute for Population Sciences (IIPS) and MoHFW.*
* **Stat 3**: *"Section 13 of PCMA empowers Judicial Magistrates of First Class to issue stay orders and injunctions against impending child marriages upon receiving any petition or complaint."*
  * **Source**: *Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006, Section 13 (Power of Court to issue injunction).*
